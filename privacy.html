Privacy

DRAFT. This text was written by the Yaanstep AI team. No lawyer has reviewed it, and it is not legal advice. It describes the app as planned; the app is not released yet. We will update this page to match what the app really does before release.

In short

  • You sign in with Google. We do not see your Google password.
  • We keep your own tasks, schedule and progress in your own space so you can use the app on your phone and on the web.
  • Analytics are optional. If you turn them on, we collect only: a random ID, an age group, a profile type, task categories and whether steps were started. Never your name, email, birth date or the words you type.
  • For ages 13 to 17, analytics are off unless you turn them on.
  • You can export or delete your data and your account at any time.

This website

This website sets no cookies. It counts visits as described under Counting visits, never who you are. It remembers your theme choice, voice settings and progress in the learning games, only in your browser. If you pick your child's age on the learning pages, it also keeps that age, a color and an optional nickname in your browser, so the right games show. Nothing is kept until you pick an age, and "Remove profile" deletes it. The avatar is a drawing made by the page; there is no photo upload. If you email us to join the test, we keep your email address on a private list and delete it when you ask.

Counting visits

To see how many people visit and which games are used most and least, this website counts visits with Umami Cloud. Umami is cookie-free and says it does not collect personal data. We count only:

  • visits, and whether a visit is new or returning;
  • which pages and games are opened, and which games are finished;
  • the age group of the child profile, if you set one: 3-4, 5-6, 7-8, 9-10 or 11-12, or "none" when there is no profile.

As standard, Umami also notes for each visit the kind of browser and device, the screen size, the language setting, the country (worked out from the connection; Umami does not store the IP address) and the website that linked here, if any.

We never send names or nicknames, answers, typed or spoken words, drawings, IDs or cookies.

To tell new visits from returning ones, the site keeps two small notes in your browser, and only while counting is on: "ys-seen" says this browser has visited before, and "ys-visit" says this visit is already counted (it is gone when you close the browser tab).

Nothing is counted if your browser asks sites not to track you, with Do Not Track or Global Privacy Control. The Yaanstep Teens pages, for ages 13 to 17, are not counted.

Reading aloud

The learning games read words aloud with a voice from your own device or browser. We pick the most natural English voice it has. In Microsoft Edge and Google Chrome the most natural voices run online, so the words being read, such as "What is 7 + 3?", go to Microsoft or Google to be spoken. Nothing about your child goes with them, and nothing your child says is recorded: there is no microphone. Where no online voice is offered, the device's own voice is used and nothing leaves the device.

Some library stories have their own recording, made with Kokoro, a free and open voice. The recordings are files on this site, so nothing goes to anyone else when they play. Stories without one use the device voice.

Tip: on an iPhone, iPad or Mac, the "Enhanced" and "Premium" voices sound much more natural and stay on the device. Download one in Settings, under Accessibility, Read and Speak (or Spoken Content), Voices, English.

Yaanstep Teens planner (early preview)

The planner keeps your events, to-dos, notes and study plans only in this browser, under one storage entry. Nothing in the planner is sent to Yaanstep or anyone else, the Teens pages are not counted, and there is no sign-in. It does not ask for your name, school, birthday or location. "Delete everything" on the planner page removes it all, and so does clearing your browser data. If we add an optional backup with sign-in later, we will update this page first and, for younger users, ask a parent where the law requires it.
