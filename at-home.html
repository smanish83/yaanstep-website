Reading games at home

Screen-free games for a grown-up and a child. They go with the reading steps (letter sounds, picture words, tap to blend). Written by Yaanstep's AI team. Not yet tried with children, and the ages are our suggestion. Nothing to sign up for and nothing is saved. You can print this page.

Keep it safe

  • A grown-up stays with the child for every game.
  • Small things can be swallowed. No buttons, beads or coins for children under 4. Use a big toy, a spoon or a ball.
  • Plastic bottles must be clean and empty, with the lid on. Use paper, not glass or metal.
  • Tape on a floor only on a dry floor away from stairs. Take it up afterwards.
  • Soft balls only. A plastic spatula, and a clean unused fly swatter or a flat hand.
  • No real names, photos of people or brand names on the cards.
  • Stop when your child is tired or upset. If an answer is wrong, say the right sound kindly and carry on.

Letter sounds

Say the sound, not the letter name: "sss" for s, not "ess".

Sticky-note sound hunt

Ages 3 to 4 · sticky notes, a pen

Write one letter on each note, such as s, t and p. Your child sticks each note on something at home that starts with that sound, like a sock, a table or a pan.

Mystery sound bag

Ages 3 to 5 · a bag, three big safe things that start with the same sound

Put in, for example, a ball, a toy bus and a toy bear. Your child looks or feels, then guesses the sound they share ("b").

Pizza flip

Ages 3 to 5 · paper circles, a pen, a plastic spatula

Write a letter on the back of each paper "pizza". Your child flips one with the spatula and says its sound. Later, write short words instead.

Letter tap

Ages 4 to 6 · 5 to 7 sticky notes with letters, a flat hand or a clean fly swatter

Stick the notes on the floor. Say a sound. Your child taps the matching letter gently.

Rhymes

Body rhymes

Ages 3 to 6 · nothing

Point to your head, then your eye, then your nose. Your child says a word that rhymes, such as bed, pie or rose. Made-up silly words count too. Some rhymes depend on how you speak, so go with your own accent.

Blending short words

Short words with three sounds, like cat or sun. Start with 4 to 6 of these: sat, pat, tap, pan, pin, tin, sit, sip, tip, nap, mat, map, dad, dig, dog, got, cat, cap, pot, kid, red, run, hat, hot, bed, bus, bug, fun, fit, hen.

Robot talk

Ages 4 to 6 · nothing

Say a word in separate sounds like a robot: "c - a - t". Your child puts the sounds together and says "cat". Then swap: your child is the robot.

Drive and read

Ages 4 to 6 · paper, a toy car

Write a word with wide gaps, like M A P. Your child drives the car slowly under each letter, saying each sound, then says the whole word.

Sound jump

Ages 4 to 6 · three paper plates

Write one letter of a word on each plate, like D, O, G. Your child hops onto each plate, says its sound, then says the whole word.

Sound hopscotch

Ages 4 to 6 · tape, word cards

Tape squares on the floor with a word card in each. Your child jumps to a square and sounds out its word.

Feed the monster

Ages 4 to 6 · a tissue box with a mouth cut in it, cards, a pen

Write a short word on each card. Your child reads a card aloud, then feeds it to the box monster.

Word bowling

Ages 4 to 6 · empty plastic bottles or cardboard tubes, tape, a soft ball

Tape a word card to each pin. Your child rolls the ball and reads the words on the pins that fall.

Letter swap

Ages 4 to 6 · paper or a whiteboard

Write a short word such as POT. Your child changes one letter to make a new word, like PIT or POD. Pick words your child knows.

Build a word

Ages 4 to 6 · one card per letter

Say a word from the list. Your child picks the letter cards and puts them in order, says each sound, then the whole word.

Picture match

Ages 4 to 6 · cards, pencil crayons

Draw a picture on one card and write its word on another: cat, dog, hat, bus, bed. Your child matches each word to its picture.

Word treasure hunt

Ages 4 to 6 · word cards, a small bag

Hide 5 word cards around the room. Your child finds one at a time, reads it, and puts it in the bag.

Memory pairs

Ages 4 to 6 · two cards for each word

Write each word on two cards and lay them face down. Your child turns two over and reads them. If they match, your child keeps the pair.
